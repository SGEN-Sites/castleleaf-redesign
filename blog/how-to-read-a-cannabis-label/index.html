How to Read a Cannabis Label, Line by Line

Cannabis 1014 min read

A cannabis label packs a lot into a small space. Here’s how to read one from top to bottom, and which numbers matter most for flower, edibles and concentrates.

In this article

  1. Small print, plain answers
  2. Reading from top to bottom
  3. Percent or milligrams?
  4. Massachusetts numbers worth knowing
  5. When the label raises a question
  6. Questions and answers

Small print, plain answers

A cannabis label can look like a page from a chemistry textbook squeezed onto a sticker. Take a breath. Once you know where to look, it reads like a recipe card. It tells you more about a product than any name or nickname ever could.

Layouts vary from brand to brand, but most labels cover the same ground. Here’s how we’d read one, top to bottom.

Reading from top to bottom

  1. 1

    Product name and type

    What it is (flower, pre-roll, vape, edible, tincture or concentrate) and its cultivar or flavor. The type tells you how the numbers below will be written.

  2. 2

    Cannabinoid content

    How much THC and CBD the product holds, plus any minor cannabinoids. Look for total THC, which also counts the THCA that becomes THC when heated.

  3. 3

    Serving information

    For edibles and tinctures: milligrams per serving, servings per package and the total for the whole package.

  4. 4

    Terpenes

    Many products list their leading terpenes, sometimes with percentages. Think of it as a preview of aroma and flavor.

  5. 5

    Ingredients

    Especially on edibles. Check for allergens the way you would with any food.

  6. 6

    Dates and batch number

    A packaging or harvest date speaks to freshness, and the batch or lot number connects the product to its testing.

  7. 7

    Warnings

    Read them every time, even on a product you know well.

Percent or milligrams?

This one trips up a lot of people. Flower, pre-rolls, vapes and concentrates usually list cannabinoids as a percentage of weight. Edibles and tinctures usually list milligrams.

  • Percentages describe concentration. A flower labeled 20% THC holds about 200 mg of THC in every gram, although how much you actually take in depends on how you consume it.
  • Milligrams describe an amount. A piece labeled 5 mg THC holds 5 mg of THC, which is why edibles are so much easier to measure.
  • Ratios describe a balance. A product marked 1:1 holds equal amounts of two cannabinoids, usually THC and CBD. Check which one is named first.

Concentrates are, as the name suggests, concentrated, so their percentages run far higher than flower’s. A small amount goes a long way.

Massachusetts numbers worth knowing

In Massachusetts, edibles are capped at 5 mg of THC a serving and 100 mg a package, so a 100 mg package of 5 mg servings holds twenty of them. When you shop, anyone 21+ with a valid government ID can buy up to 1 oz (28 grams) of flower equivalent per day and no more than 5 grams of concentrates.

When the label raises a question

Labels are good at facts and less good at context. They can tell you how much THC a flower holds, but not whether it suits a quiet Sunday with a sketchbook. That’s where people come in.

Bring your questions to the counter. Our budtenders are happy to read a label with you and explain what each number means for the way you like to enjoy cannabis. You belong here, whether it’s your first label or your five hundredth.

Questions and answers

What does total THC mean?
It’s the THC a product will hold once heated: the THC already present plus the THC its THCA will become. On flower, it’s the number to compare.
Why do edibles use milligrams and flower use percentages?
Edibles come in measured servings, so milligrams tell you exactly what’s in each piece. Flower is used in varying amounts, so a percentage of weight is the clearer way to describe its strength.
What is the batch number for?
It ties the product to a specific production run and its testing. Keep the packaging until you’ve finished the product, in case a question comes up later.
